# ICP and personas, implementers

Load for implementer targeting, outreach lists, session applications, and partner copy. Distills the implementer ICP Dashboard and Buying Committee (2026-11-03). Bands are planning assumptions asked in the session application (D55). Implementers are segments and counts, never a named prospect list.

## The two levels

| | Level 1: no AI shopping assistant in their build | Level 2: has deployed AI chat, wants something repeatable |
|---|---|---|
| Segment | Freelance Shopify developers and small studios who install apps for client stores, including on Upwork and Fiverr | Build and CRO agencies at Select or Plus tier and above that have set up AI chat for several clients |
| Location | Serving US stores, based anywhere | US-serving first, then UK, Canada, Australia. US directory, March 2026: 33 Platinum, 45 Premier, 255 Plus, 342 Select (secondary) |
| Bands (planning) | 1 to 5 people, under $250,000 a year, 2 to 10 active client stores, Registered or unlisted | 6 to 50 people, $1 million to $20 million, 10 to 100 client stores |
| How they charge | Fixed gigs and hourly. AI chatbot gigs $30 to $1,000, app installs about $100 | Projects and retainers, CRO fees sometimes tied to results |
| Pain | Income gig by gig, no credential beside the apps they install, no paid time to learn an app, clients ask about AI and they have no tested answer | Every app they recommend becomes their support burden. Programs that pay only after referrals. Theme residue. No proof to show for AI chat |
| Triggers | A client asks for an AI chatbot, an offer of paid work, Shopify AI news | An unhappy chat client, a redesign, next quarter's service lines, Shopify Editions and DotDev |
| Engagement | The paid session as a job on Upwork or Fiverr, or by email and LinkedIn | The paid session by email and LinkedIn, then a case study, then the partnership |

## The committee

**Level 1 collapses into the freelancer, and the merchant client pays.** The freelancer decides whether to take the session and whether to recommend Pico. Blocks: a fee below their time, unclear scope, fear of breaking a client store, a platform rule that risks their account, no reviews of Pico. Drivers: paid work on first contact, a tested AI answer, credit in a case study, early access to the partnership. Assets: the job post with fixed scope and fee, the sandbox, the setup skill, the technical one-pager. The client approves any install on their store and must see the findings list, the setup time, and that Pico installs with no theme code to edit. Pico never contacts the client directly.

**Level 2 holds, with the merchant client as budget holder.**

## Design partners: the paid usability cohort
Implementers serving US stores, reachable on Upwork, Fiverr, email, or LinkedIn, willing to install Pico on a development store or a permissioned client store, give structured feedback, join an interview, and consider a case study. Current tools: Shopify CLI, the theme editor, development stores, coding agents for some, and the apps agencies list most (Klaviyo, Gorgias, Recharge, Okendo, Rebuy).

Targets: 30 sessions by day 60, median setup under 30 minutes, 15 interviews, 3 case study permissions from both sides, 15 partner sign-ups by day 90.

How it runs: 30 paid sessions in 60 days from week 1 (Upwork 15, Fiverr 5, email 6, LinkedIn 4). Fee $150, paid on the platform where found, or by invoice within 7 days of the completed feedback form, with a session agreement and a W-9 or W-8BEN. Interview optional, 30 minutes, $75. Case studies only with recorded permission from merchant and implementer. The partnership offer is open through email, LinkedIn, and Pico's own pages, never inside Fiverr, and Upwork partners are paid through Upwork for 24 months. Exit at 30 sessions (week 9) or the first permissioned case study, then batches of 10 under the runway gate.

Early adopter traits: has installed AI chat for clients, uses coding agents, replies quickly, willing to be credited in public.
