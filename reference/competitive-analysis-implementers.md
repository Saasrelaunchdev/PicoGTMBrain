Implementers are analyzed as segments and counts, never as a named prospect list. Program terms change on notice and several pages conflict.

Watchlist, 13 names: Rep AI, Zipchat, Tidio, Intercom, Octane, Fast Simon, Nosto, Recharge, Okendo, LoyaltyLion, Voiceflow, Chatbase, and freelance gigs as a segment.

## What implementers value and resent
- Value: a vendor tier the agency can display (11 of 26 sampled pages show tiers), integration with the client's core stack, leads from vendor directories, paid implementation work.
- Resent: commission confusion and payout problems, requirements that grow, the support burden when a recommended app misbehaves, app sprawl and theme residue after uninstall.

## Where each leader wins
- Partner economics (Shopify Partner Program): a known share and a tier make yes easy.
- Stack presence (Klaviyo): the app already in the client stack wins by default.
- Paid first (build today, Pico by design): paying for the first session turns a cold message into paid work, the reason to reply.
- Co-owned proof (build today, Pico by design): a credited case study is the implementer's marketing, so they stay.

## The only element no offer has
The paid usability engagement. Every program found pays after a referral or a closed deal. Planned, not running, so it is never described as established.

## Platform rules that shape the route
Upwork: a 24-month non-circumvention term on relationships found there. Fiverr: no off-platform payment or steering. LinkedIn: no automation. CAN-SPAM: sender identity, advertisement notice, postal address, opt-out within 10 business days. Detail: `outreach-engine.md`.
