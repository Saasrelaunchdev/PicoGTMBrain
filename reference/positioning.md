# Positioning statements, both audiences

Load for any headline, tagline, page hero, or pitch. Distills the two positioning dashboards (Dashboard 3 per audience, 2026-11-03).

## Merchants

**Core claim.** Pico shows merchants what their shoppers ask for, and answers shoppers with products drawn from the store's own synced catalog.

**1. Against AI sales chat and support agents.** Not "another AI chatbot that tries to answer everything", a shopping assistant built around your synced catalog that shows you what shoppers asked for. Shoppers describe what they need in their own words, and Pico replies with product cards showing names, prices, and stock from the synced catalog. The merchant sees what shoppers asked for, what they clicked and added to cart, and the phrases that returned no products or few clicks.
- Basis: support automation breadth is where agents lead (vector 2), insight into what shoppers ask is where the slate scores lowest (vector 3).
- Must not drift into: "never makes things up", "the only assistant that sticks to your catalog", "increases sales".

**2. Against search, quiz, and recommendation tools, and custom builds.** Not "a smarter search bar", a conversation for shoppers who don't know a product's name. Search, filters, quizzes, and widgets stay where they are. Pico adds a place to describe the need, one clarifying question when a request is vague, product cards from the synced catalog, and one tap to the store's own cart. It installs from the App Store with a four-step checklist.
- Basis: Pico has no semantic search, filters, or recommendation widgets, so it sits beside search.
- Must not drift into: any comparison with Shopify search, "understands what shoppers mean", "semantic search", "checkout in chat", "no developer needed".

**3. Against doing nothing.** Not "one more app to manage", a low-risk way to see what your shoppers are asking for. The theme, search, and collections stay. Pico adds a widget through a theme app embed, with no theme code to edit. Try the sandbox first, then start on Free, 50 conversations a month, with a 14 day Growth trial and no card.
- Must not drift into: "free forever", "unlimited", "zero setup", "your store is losing sales".

## Implementers

**Core claim.** Pico pays implementers for a first session on a store they work on, and, with the client's permission, credits their work in the case study that follows.

**1. Against AI assistant vendors with agency programs.** Not "another affiliate program that pays after the referral", a paid session that starts before any referral exists. We pay you $150 to install Pico on a store you work on and tell us what works and what breaks. With your client's written permission, we write up what changed on their store and credit your work. Participants are invited to the partner program: 25% of each referred store's subscription for 24 months.
- Basis: every program found pays after a referral or a closed deal (vector 3), and none credits the implementer as a standard element (vector 4).
- Must not drift into: "earn recurring revenue", "join the Pico Partner Program", "certified partner", "white label", "guaranteed leads".

**2. Against custom builds, the free native agent, and the standard stack.** Not "a build you have to maintain", an app you set up in four steps or hand to your coding agent. Pico installs through a theme app embed, and Shopify removes app embed blocks on uninstall. The client gets an Analytics page listing the phrases shoppers asked that returned no products or few clicks.
- Must not drift into: "installs itself", "zero setup", any speed claim beyond the published Lighthouse result, anything about what the native agent lacks.

**3. Against offering no AI shopping help.** Not "a new service line to build from scratch", a paid first session on a store you already work on. Try the sandbox first, then run a paid session and leave with the phrases its shoppers asked that the catalog could not answer.
- Basis: no AI shopping assistant appears on 26 sampled agency stack pages, and freelancers already sell app installs for about $100.
- Must not drift into: "a new revenue stream", "your clients will sell more".

## What would change these statements
- Revenue attribution or holdout measurement ships and produces data: an outcome line becomes possible, only after it enters the proof log.
- Human handoff ships: the "tries to answer everything" contrast weakens.
- A slate competitor ships a conversational zero-result report: lead with the fix loop in `positioning-verdict.md`.
- The partner terms change, or the multi-store workspace ships: implementer statements quote the new terms.
- The first case study has recorded permission: a proof line in its exact permitted wording.
