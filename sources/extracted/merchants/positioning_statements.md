# Run 1, Dashboard 3: Positioning statements, Shopify merchants

Step 3.1 of gtm-builder v3.1, for the primary market: Shopify merchants, global, USA first. Built on 2026-11-03 from Dashboard 1 (demand driver) and Dashboard 2 (vectors 3 and 4, where Pico can lead).

**Rules applied.** The skill's template joins its two halves with a dash. Here a comma does that job. Claims use the narrowed wording in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5. No statement names a competitor, and none says what Shopify or its native tools lack: the status quo statement is about doing nothing beyond what the store already has.

## Core claim

Pico shows merchants what their shoppers ask for, and answers shoppers with products drawn from the store's own synced catalog.

## The three statements

### 1. Against AI sales chat and support agents (direct competitors)

**Not "another AI chatbot that tries to answer everything", a shopping assistant built around your synced catalog that shows you what shoppers asked for.** Shoppers describe what they need in their own words, and Pico replies with product cards showing names, prices, and stock from the synced catalog. The merchant sees what shoppers asked for, what they clicked and added to cart, and the phrases that returned no products or few clicks.

- **Basis:** Dashboard 2, Vectors_2.8. Support automation breadth is where AI sales and support agents lead (vector 2), and insight into what shoppers ask is where the slate scores lowest (vector 3).
- **Must not drift into:** "never makes things up", "the only assistant that sticks to your catalog", "increases sales".

### 2. Against search, quiz, and recommendation tools, and custom builds (indirect competitors and alternatives)

**Not "a smarter search bar", a conversation for shoppers who don't know a product's name.** Search, filters, quizzes, and recommendation widgets stay where they are. Pico adds a place where shoppers can describe what they need, get one clarifying question when the request is vague, see product cards from the synced catalog, and add to the store's own cart in one tap. It installs from the App Store with a four-step checklist, so there is nothing to build or maintain.

- **Basis:** Dashboard 2, Presence_2.10 rows 19, 23, and 24: Pico has no semantic search, filters, merchandising, or recommendation widgets, so it adds a conversation beside search rather than replacing it. The build or hire profile (slot 5) carries the cost of building and maintaining the same thing.
- **Must not drift into:** any comparison with Shopify search, "understands what shoppers mean", "semantic search", "checkout in chat", "no developer needed".

### 3. Against the status quo: doing nothing beyond what the store already has

**Not "one more app to manage", a low-risk way to see what your shoppers are asking for.** The store keeps its theme, search, and collections. Pico adds a widget through a theme app embed, with no theme code to edit. The merchant can try the shopper experience in a sandbox store before installing anything, then start on the free plan, which covers 50 conversations a month, with a 14 day Growth trial and no card.

- **Basis:** Dashboard 1, demand driver "Shopper Words Test" and the sandbox (row 19), which no competitor sampled in Step 0.A was found to offer. Dashboard 2, Potency_2.12 rows 15 and 16.
- **Must not drift into:** "free forever", "unlimited", "zero setup", "your store is losing sales", anything about what Shopify's tools lack.
