# Run 1, Dashboard 6: Value Proposition, Shopify merchants

Steps 6.1 and 6.2 of gtm-builder v3.1 for the primary market, USA first. Built on 2026-11-03 from Dashboards 1 to 5.

**Rules applied in every table below.**

- The proof set is empty and closed for customer proof (`GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5). In all nine copy drills, the template's Social Proof headline is written as an **Open Proof headline**, an invitation to check the claim now, and the **Supporting Proof Point** names something the reader can check, and where (`DECISIONS.md` D4). Each drill keeps its **Method or process line**. Open proof is never presented as a customer result.
- Every claim uses the narrowed wording in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5. Nothing says sales, revenue, conversion, or ROI about Pico. No competitor is named and Shopify's tools are never described.
- Body copy is 25 words or fewer, counted.
- CTAs that point to the App Store read "Connect your store" and link to picofinds.com until the listing is live, planned for week 3 (`DECISIONS.md` D2).

## Step 6.1: Brand promise and positioning

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| Brand Promise | Shoppers find products from your catalog by asking in their own words, and you see what they asked for |
| Positioning | Built around your synced catalog, with insight into what shoppers ask: Pico shows the phrases shoppers used, how requests move from product results to clicks and add to cart, and which phrases returned nothing or drew few clicks. Phase 2 found this lead narrow, so it is stated as what Pico is built around, never as something only Pico can do |

## When customer proof replaces open proof

A customer proof point replaces the open proof in its drill only from a row in the proof set, in its exact permitted wording, with its source and recorded permission (`GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5, intake rule). The first candidates are the founding cohort, asked in writing at day 60 (Dashboard 5, D30), and the case studies from Run 2's paid sessions, requested from week 9.
