# SLIDE 1, The Pico GTM dashboard suite for Shopify implementers

- Pico is an AI shopping assistant for Shopify stores. This go-to-market is for the people who set stores up: agencies of every kind, Shopify developers, theme developers, and freelancers
- A standalone go-to-market, US-serving implementers first, wherever they are based
- Core claim: Pico pays implementers for a first session on a store they work on, and, with the client's permission, credits their work in the case study that follows
- Channels: cold email, LinkedIn by hand, and Upwork and Fiverr job posts
- Evidence state: decided, not yet run. $150 a session, 25% of referred stores for 24 months, sessions from week 1, the partner page from week 6
- So what: the first contact is an offer of paid work, not a pitch

# SLIDE 2, Product Feature Matrix Dashboard, what we learned and what we lead with

- Seven implementer-facing product rows, three candidates, and twelve program elements, scored on demand and competitiveness
- Table stakes Pico lacks: a recurring share, development store installs, a directory listing, tier badges
- Hero candidates to build: a multi-store workspace, case studies credited to the implementer, pay for managing client stores
- Differentiators today: the sandbox store, the coding agent setup skill, and the paid usability engagement, which no vendor found offers
- Demand driver: the Paid Usability Session
- So what: the opening offer pays for the implementer's time, so replying costs them nothing

# SLIDE 3, Competitive Market Analysis Dashboard, competitor map and buying context

- Slate: Gorgias, Shopify Inbox with the Inbox agent, Rebuy plus Search & Discovery, Klaviyo Customer Agent, a custom build with Shopify's AI Toolkit, and the Shopify Partner Program
- No AI shopping assistant appears on 26 sampled agency stack pages. Gorgias and Klaviyo hold the seat where it is held at all
- Every program found pays after a referral or a closed deal, mostly around 20% for 12 to 36 months
- Implementers complain about payout problems, requirements that grow, and the support burden of apps they recommend
- Upwork's 24-month non-circumvention term and Fiverr's off-platform ban shape how a partnership can start
- So what: Pico enters where programs are weakest, before the referral

# SLIDE 5, ICP Dashboard, who we win first, and why

- Level 1: freelancers and small studios who install apps for clients, many on Upwork and Fiverr, with no AI shopping assistant in their build
- Level 2: build and CRO agencies that have deployed AI chat before and want something repeatable across client stores
- US Partner Directory agencies, March 2026: 33 Platinum, 45 Premier, 255 Plus, 342 Select, per an agency-published guide
- Fiverr shows 40,000+ Shopify gigs. Upwork publishes no headcount. Client stores per implementer: 2 to 10 for freelancers, 10 to 100 for agencies, planning bands asked at application (D55)
- Gulf agencies are shown for later and not pursued now
- So what: freelancers already sell app installs for about $100, so a paid session reads as ordinary work

# SLIDE 10, Pricing and Packaging Dashboard, value metric, usage metric, tiers, expansion levers

- Launch metric: a fee per paid usability session, $150 for up to 90 minutes, plus $75 for an optional interview
- Primary partner metric: 25% of each referred store's subscription for 24 months, 30% for lead partners, 10% on managed stores
- Ladder: session participant, partner, lead partner at 5 active stores, and a free agency workspace as the anchor
- No separate agency price: Shopify Billing charges each store, so the workspace is free and built once 10 partners each have 3 or more active stores (D23, D41)
- Expansion levers: more client stores, tier progression, a managed share, case studies
- So what: the session fee buys the first installs, and the recurring share keeps the implementer
