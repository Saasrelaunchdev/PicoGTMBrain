# Run 2, Dashboard 3: Positioning statements, Shopify implementers

Step 3.1 of gtm-builder v3.1, run standalone for implementers (owner decision, 2026-11-03): agencies of every kind, Shopify developers, theme developers, and freelancers, US-serving first. Built on 2026-11-03 from Dashboard 1 (the Paid Usability Session) and Dashboard 2 (vectors 3 and 4).

**Rules applied.** A comma does the job of the skill's template dash. The fee and the partner terms are decided, $150 a session and 25% for 24 months (`DECISIONS.md` D33, D35), and statements quote them exactly. No volume of traffic is ever stated (D39). Every statement uses the narrowed Run 2 wording in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5. No statement names a competitor, and none says what Shopify or its tools lack.

## Core claim

Pico pays implementers for a first session on a store they work on, and, with the client's permission, credits their work in the case study that follows.

## The three statements

### 1. Against AI assistant vendors with agency programs (direct competitors)

**Not "another affiliate program that pays after the referral", a paid session that starts before any referral exists.** We pay you $150 to install Pico on a store you work on and tell us what works and what breaks. With your client's written permission, we write up what changed on their store and credit your work. Participants are invited to the partner program: 25% of each referred store's subscription for 24 months.

- **Basis:** Dashboard 2, Vectors_2.8. Every program found pays after a referral or a closed deal (vector 3), and none credits the implementer in a case study built with their client store as a standard element (vector 4). Dashboard 1: P1 is the only element no offer has.
- **Must not drift into:** "earn recurring revenue", "join the Pico Partner Program", "certified partner", "white label", "guaranteed leads", any rate or traffic number.

### 2. Against custom builds, the free native agent, and the standard discovery stack (indirect competitors and alternatives)

**Not "a build you have to maintain", an app you set up in four steps or hand to your coding agent.** Pico installs through a theme app embed, so there is no theme code to edit, and Shopify removes app embed blocks when an app is uninstalled. Pico gives developers a sandbox and a setup skill their coding agent can follow. Your client gets an Analytics page that lists the phrases shoppers asked that returned no products or few clicks.

- **Basis:** Dashboard 2, Potency_2.12 rows 16 to 19, and the build profile: maintenance and migrations stay with the builder. Shopify's documentation on theme app extensions for uninstall behavior.
- **Must not drift into:** "installs itself", "zero setup", "no developer needed", any speed claim beyond the published Lighthouse result (D18), anything about what the native agent or Shopify's search lacks.

### 3. Against the status quo: offering no AI shopping help to clients

**Not "a new service line to build from scratch", a paid first session on a store you already work on.** Try the shopper experience in the sandbox store first, with no client store involved. Then run a paid session on a store you work on, and leave with a list of the phrases its shoppers asked that the catalog could not answer, which you can take to your client.

- **Basis:** Dashboard 1, the Paid Usability Session and the Client Store Findability Audit variant. Step 0.F: no AI shopping assistant appears in the 26 sampled agency stacks, and freelancers already sell app installs for about $100.
- **Must not drift into:** "a new revenue stream", "your clients will sell more", any claim about the client's results.
