# Run 2, Dashboard 5: Buying Committee, Shopify implementers

Steps 5.1 and 5.2 of gtm-builder v3.1 for the two implementer ICP levels in Dashboard 4. Built on 2026-11-03. Following `SKILL-ADAPTATION.md` Phase 5, Run 2: the committee holds for agencies (developer, project or account lead, agency owner), and the merchant client is the budget holder behind all three, so each table records what the implementer must show the client to justify the line item. For Level 1 freelancers the committee collapses into one person.

"To test" marks a channel with no evidence in this run's research. Platform rules come from `research/2026-11-03-run-2-channel-rules.md`.

## Sheet: Design_Partners

### Step 5.2: the paid usability cohort

The owner's path makes this cohort the design partner group for Run 2: implementers recruited by email, LinkedIn, Upwork, and Fiverr, paid for a usability session, then interviewed, then invited to build a case study with a client store. It is also where the proof capture rules in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5 attach to real accounts, including the client stores implementers bring.

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Design Partner Attributes | Implementers who serve US Shopify stores and install apps for clients, reachable on Upwork, Fiverr, email, or LinkedIn, willing to install Pico on a store they work on (a development store, or a client store with the merchant's permission), give structured feedback, join an interview, and consider a case study |
| Job Titles | Freelance Shopify developer, studio developer, agency developer, technical lead, CRO specialist. Agency owner for Level 2 |
| Demographics | Based anywhere, serving US stores. English as a working language. 1 to 5 people, under $250,000 a year, 2 to 10 active client stores, planning bands asked in the application (D55) |
| Responsibility Areas | App installs, theme work, client store maintenance |
| Current Tools | Shopify CLI and the theme editor. Development stores in the Partner Dashboard. Coding agents for some. The apps agencies list most: Klaviyo, Gorgias, Recharge, Okendo, Rebuy (Step 0.F) |
| Key Pain Points | Paid gig by gig. Programs that pay late or only after a referral. The support burden of apps they recommend. Nothing to show for AI work |
| Critical Triggers | A client asks for an AI chatbot. An offer of paid work with a clear scope |
| Goals & Motivations | Paid work. Early access. Credit in a case study. Shaping a product. Partnership terms when they exist |
| Decision Criteria | The fee against the time asked: $150 for up to 90 minutes (D33). A clear scope. Payment through the platform they use. Setup time. Whether the client agrees |
| Influence Level | The freelancer decides alone. The agency owner approves on Level 2. The client approves any install on their store |
| Barriers to Purchase | Pico has no reviews or reputation yet. Client permission. Platform rules on relationships that move off Upwork and Fiverr. Time |
| Information Sources | Upwork and Fiverr feeds, LinkedIn, the Shopify Developer Community, Partners Slack, r/shopify |
| Success Metrics | Sessions completed, setup time recorded, feedback items logged, interviews held, case study permissions recorded, partnership sign-ups. Targets: 30 sessions by day 60, median setup under 30 minutes, 15 interviews, 3 case study permissions from both sides, 15 partner sign-ups by day 90 (D45) |
| Early Adopter Characteristics | Has installed AI chat apps for clients before, uses coding agents, replies quickly, and is willing to be credited in public |

### How the cohort runs

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Cohort size | 30 paid sessions in 60 days from week 1: Upwork 15, Fiverr 5, email 6, LinkedIn 4 (D37) |
| Recruitment | A fixed-price job post on Upwork. On Fiverr, ordering an existing gig or posting a brief. Cold email, with the CAN-SPAM requirements. LinkedIn messages sent by hand, with no automation |
| Fee | $150 (D33), paid through Upwork or Fiverr when the implementer was found there, and by invoice within 7 days of the completed feedback form when found by email or LinkedIn. Pico pays the platform fees on top. Invoice-paid implementers sign a one-page session agreement and give a W-9 or W-8BEN (D52) |
| Session scope | Sandbox first. Then install on a development store or on a client store with the merchant's permission. Run the Shopper Words Test. Log setup time. Fill in the feedback form. Up to 90 minutes of the implementer's time (D33) |
| Interview | Optional, 30 minutes, $75, booked after the session: a second milestone on Upwork, a separate order on Fiverr (D34) |
| Case study | Only with recorded permission from the merchant and the implementer, entered in the proof set under the intake rule |
| Partnership offer | Made only where the platform allows it. Upwork: a 24-month non-circumvention term covers relationships found there. Fiverr: no off-platform payments or steering. So the offer is open through email, LinkedIn, and Pico's own pages, where an implementer can join on their own. Upwork partners are paid through Upwork for 24 months, and no invitation ever goes inside Fiverr (D49). **Counsel reviews the terms and the conduct rules in week 5, before the partner page goes live in week 6** (D50) |
| Exit | When 30 sessions are complete, planned for week 9, or when the first case study clears the intake rule, whichever comes first (D37). After that, sessions run in batches of 10, funded block by block under the runway gate (D32) |
