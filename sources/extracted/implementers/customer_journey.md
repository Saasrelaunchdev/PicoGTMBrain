## Sheet: Engagement

### Stage 4, the paid usability engagement, step by step

Each step has a drop-off question, the platform rule that applies, and a target in the Targets sheet (D45).

| Step | What happens | Drop-off question | Platform rule or blocker | Intervention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Recruit | A fixed-price job post on Upwork, a brief or a gig order on Fiverr, a cold email, or a LinkedIn message | Did the right implementers see it? | Email: CAN-SPAM requires accurate headers, an ad identification, a postal address, and an opt-out honored within 10 business days, with no B2B exception. LinkedIn: no automation tools | Scope and fee in the first line. Messages sent by hand |
| 2. Accept | The implementer accepts the scope and the $150 fee (D33) | Is the fee worth their time? | Upwork: payments for relationships found there stay on Upwork for 24 months, unless a conversion fee is paid. Fiverr: payment only by a Fiverr order | Pay on the platform where they were found |
| 3. Sandbox test | The implementer tries the sandbox store and the setup skill | Did they get stuck before reaching a real store? | None | Setup walkthrough video |
| 4. Client permission | If a client store is used, the merchant agrees in writing | Will the client say yes? | The client is the budget holder | A one-page permission template that says what Pico does and does not do. A development store as the fallback |
| 5. Install | Four-step checklist or the setup skill: authorize, enable the theme embed, sync, confirm the widget was seen | Was the embed switched on, on the live theme? | Shopify app embeds are off by default after install | Checklist prompts, and a setup-time log |
| 6. Shopper Words Test | The implementer types phrases from the client's customers and reads the Analytics page | Did any phrase return nothing? | None | The findings template |
| 7. Feedback | A structured form: what worked, what broke, setup time | Was the feedback specific enough to act on? | None | Required fields in the form |
| 8. Payment | The fee is paid | Was it on time? | As step 2 | Pay within 7 days of the completed feedback form, or release the milestone or order on the platform (D33) |
| 9. Interview | Optional, with its own incentive | Did they have time? | Same platform rule as step 2 | Offer it inside the same engagement |
| 10. Case study permission | Optional, from the merchant and the implementer | Will both agree? | The proof set's intake rule | A permission form with the exact wording to be published |
| 11. Partnership invite | The offer to join the partner program: 25% for 24 months, 30% for lead partners (D35), from week 6 | Will they join? | Fiverr: no steering off the platform, so no invitation inside Fiverr. Upwork: payouts within 24 months may count as circumvention | Open the program on Pico's own page and by email or LinkedIn, where implementers join on their own. Upwork partners are paid through Upwork for 24 months (D49). Counsel review in week 5 (D50) |

## Sheet: Insights

### Critical Success Factors

| # | Factor |
|---|---|
| 1 | A fee and scope that match the implementer's time, stated in the first message: $150 for up to 90 minutes (D33) |
| 2 | The install works first time on a real store, because the first session is also the first impression |
| 3 | Client permission handled up front, for installs on client stores and for case studies |
| 4 | Partnership terms ready before the first sessions end, because the programs implementers already know set the floor: a share, a tier, a directory |

### Primary Journey Blockers

| # | Blocker |
|---|---|
| 1 | Platform rules: Upwork's 24-month non-circumvention term and Fiverr's ban on off-platform payment and steering |
| 2 | A new program with no track record. The terms are set (D35 to D40), but no partner has been paid yet, so the first payouts on the 15th must be on time |
| 3 | Client permission for installs and for case studies |
| 4 | One store per plan: with no multi-store workspace (candidate C3), each repeat deployment is a separate plan |

### Acceleration Opportunities

| # | Opportunity |
|---|---|
| 1 | Start with freelancers who already sell app installs for about $100, where a paid session reads as ordinary work |
| 2 | Shorten every session with the sandbox and the setup skill |
| 3 | Turn the findings list into a service the implementer can sell, the Client Store Findability Audit |
| 4 | Paid plans free for testing on development stores (C11), switched on in Shopify App Pricing before outreach starts (D22) |
