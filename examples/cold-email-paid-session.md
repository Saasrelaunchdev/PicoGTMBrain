# Example, first cold email (implementers, stage 1)

Status: proposed gold standard, under 120 words in the body. US recipients only in the first 90 days. Founder approval pending.

---

**Subject:** Paid: $150 to test an AI shopping app on a store you work on

Hi [first name],

I'm [name], founder of Pico, an AI shopping assistant for Shopify stores.

We pay you $150 to install Pico on a store you work on and tell us what works and what breaks.

The scope is fixed, up to 90 minutes: try our sandbox store, install on a development store or a client store with the merchant's written permission, run the Shopper Words Test, log your setup time, and fill in a short feedback form. We pay by invoice within 7 days of the completed form.

Reply "in" and I'll send the session brief.

[Name]
Pico, picofinds.com

This is an advertisement. [US postal address]. Reply "stop" or [unsubscribe] and you won't hear from us again.

---

## Why this works
- The offer, the fee, and the scope are in the first three lines. It reads as paid work, not a pitch.
- It asks for something the implementer already does: install an app and report back.
- No partner rates, no income promises, no traffic claims, which is the first-email rule.
- The CAN-SPAM footer is part of the template, not an afterthought.
